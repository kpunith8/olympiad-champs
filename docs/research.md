## Implications

- The intended model is **free learning**; the owner is considering premium practice and tests. Competitors commonly provide free assessment samples, so a limited free practice set and representative mock could let families evaluate explanations before paying. This is a proposal, not an approved entitlement.
- Paid value could be a reviewed question bank, worked explanations, multiple genuinely distinct tests, accurate results and reliable resume. Competitors already offer several of these; differentiation and willingness to pay need validation.
- Consider a simple non-recurring subject or all-subject season pass. Confirm class, child, expiry, taxes, refunds and entitlement scope before pricing. Competitor prices cover different products and are not a market average or recommended price. Do not promise multiple distinct full tests with only 35 questions.
- Design purchase for an adult, disclose restrictions before an activity starts, and preserve access to existing learner data. Do not use child-directed purchase pressure, targeted ads or invented results. Decide parent/child accounts and consent before real-user launch.

## Validate

Interview parents and learners with appropriate consent; test whether the free/premium boundary is understandable and whether the content earns trust. Compare subject and all-subject packages and explicit expiry dates; stated purchase intent is not actual conversion. Calculate authoring, review, hosting, payment, tax, support and refund costs before pricing. For illustration only, 100 × ₹499 = ₹49,900 gross, before all costs; this is not a forecast.

Open decisions: free assessment allowance; price, duration and scope; number of reviewed questions/tests; exam patterns and content rights; purchaser, consent, refunds and access after expiry. Recheck offers before making commercial decisions.
